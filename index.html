Included

Three ways to browse, out of the box

Same apps, three different shapes. Switch with one button in the drawer's toolbar; search works in all of them, and any app can be long-pressed and dragged onto your home screen from any of them.

Rainbow

Every app in one list, grouped into color sections from red through violet. A toggle hides the headers for one continuous rainbow.

Mosaic

A mosaic of color tiles, one per color, each filled with that color's icons. Tap a tile to open those apps as a list.

Honeycomb

A hex spiral, one cell per app, with the apps you use most in the center. Grant usage access and it reorders itself around you.

Underneath

And a real home screen

A color-sorted drawer is no use bolted to a launcher that can't hold a widget. This one does the ordinary things properly.

  • Pages, folders and a dock, with drag-and-drop straight out of any drawer view.
  • Widgets, with resizing by a corner grip and a choice of picker layouts.
  • Search from the drawer, in every view.
  • Uninstall or open app info straight from an icon.
  • Notification badges on icons — red or icon-complementary, three sizes, with an optional breathing glow. Needs notification access; off by default.
  • Back up your layout to a file you choose, and restore it later.

Privacy

Nothing of yours leaves your phone

No ads, no analytics, no accounts, no tracking. Hue-istic's own code asks for no network permission and never transmits anything, anywhere.

Your app list stays local

Icons are read from the system and the color worked out for each one is cached on your device. It is never uploaded.

Usage access is optional

Granted by you in Android settings, used only to order apps by use, held in memory, never written to disk. Revoke it and every view falls back to unsorted.

Badges read two fields

Which app posted a notification, and whether it is an ongoing one. Never the title, the text or any other content — and nothing is kept once it is dismissed.

Crash reports stay put

Written to the app's own storage so a problem can be diagnosed. The app never sends them; sharing one is a thing you do, through Android's share sheet.

One caveat, stated plainly: Google's Play Billing library — the thing that will handle the single optional purchase — brings the internet permission with it and may send Google its own diagnostics about how the library is working. That is the only network code in the app, none of it is ours, and none of it is about you.
